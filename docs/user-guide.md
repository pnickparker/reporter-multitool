# Mobile News Bureau — user guide

*Working name. Draft written 2026-10-14 from how the app behaves today; screen wording was checked against the code. Replace `[support email]` before this goes to anyone outside the test group. Re-read it against the live app before each release.*

Mobile News Bureau turns a recording into things you can use: a **transcript**, the **best quotes** (checked against the transcript), and **draft social posts**. It's built for a reporter who is out in the field with a phone.

**One rule to remember: your recording is never lost on purpose.** If something goes wrong, nothing is thrown away — you tap **Try again**, you don't re-record.

---

## 1. Get started (two minutes)

1. Open the web address your editor gave you in **Safari** (iPhone) or **Chrome** (Android) and enter the passphrase.
2. **Add it to your home screen** so it opens like an app:
   - **iPhone:** tap the Share button → **Add to Home Screen**.
   - **Android:** tap the browser menu → **Add to Home screen** / **Install app**.
3. Do step 2 *before* you start working. The home-screen version asks for the passphrase again and keeps its own storage, so anything you started in the browser won't be there.

## 2. Capture something

On the Home screen you have three ways to bring something in.

| Button | Use it for |
|---|---|
| **Big circle** | Opens your camera. Take a photo or record a video; it uploads on its own when you finish. |
| **Upload** | Anything already on your phone: video, audio, photos, PDFs. |
| **Note** | A typed or dictated note (tap the microphone on your keyboard to dictate). |

**For an interview, record audio with your phone's own voice recorder app, then Upload it.** That's the most reliable way to record a long conversation. The in-app camera is best for quick photos and video.

**Camera clips can stop at about 10 minutes.** On an iPhone, a video recorded through the big circle ends by itself at roughly 10 minutes and goes straight to the upload screen. That's the phone's limit, not an error, and nothing is lost: the clip is saved in full. Start a new clip to keep going. For a long interview, use your phone's own camera or voice recorder (which have no such cap), then **Upload** the file.

**Know this about the camera:** pictures and video taken through the big circle go to the app, **not** to your phone's Photos. The app keeps a copy on your phone until the upload finishes. If you want one in Photos too, use **Save to phone** (see below).

**Turn off Low Power Mode before you shoot.** The camera keeps your screen awake while you record, so you can hold the phone still and point it without the screen locking. Low Power Mode can override that: it makes the phone lock the screen after 30 seconds and may stop the app from keeping it awake, which can end a video partway through. (If **Auto-Lock** is greyed out in Settings → Display & Brightness, Low Power Mode is on.) If a video does stop short, keep it rather than tapping Retake. What was recorded is still there.

Everything you add goes into a **project** — a folder for one story or event. Quick capture makes one for you; you can rename it any time by tapping its title.

## 3. While it uploads

- You'll see a percentage. **Keep the app open until it finishes** if you can, and keep the screen awake — a locked phone can pause an upload.
- **If your signal drops**, the app waits and retries by itself, and picks up where it stopped. You don't need to do anything.
- **If you see Try again, tap it.** It resumes with the same file. Do **not** record it again.
- **If you quit the app mid-upload,** open it again: it shows **Resume upload** for what was started. A copy stays on your phone until the upload is complete.
- **Save to phone** puts a copy in your Photos/Files. Use it if you're worried about a bad connection and want your own copy.
- A big or old video that lives in **iCloud** can sit on "preparing…" for several minutes before the upload starts. That's the phone downloading it first, not the app.

## 4. What happens next

When a recording finishes uploading it's processed automatically:

1. **Transcription** — spoken words turned into text, with who spoke.
2. **Best moments** — the AI picks quotable lines and scores them.
3. **Post drafts** — short social posts for the strongest quotes.

You'll see the steps, a timer and how long it usually takes. **You can close the app — it keeps going.** Open the project later and it's done.

Typical times: a short clip usually takes a minute or two; a long interview can take a few minutes (a 35-minute video took under three).

### Length and size limits

- Roughly **40 minutes of video** or **1½ hours of audio** is processed automatically.
- **Size matters as much as minutes** — 4K video is enormous.
- A bigger file is still saved safely. It shows an amber **Saved, not transcribed**. Tap **Transcribe now** to try anyway; it often works.
- If a recording is too big to process, it stays in your project, and you can still share or open the original.

## 5. Check and fix the transcript

- **Speaker labels are a best guess.** Look at them before you publish anything.
- **Give a speaker a real name:** under the transcript, tap "Speaker A" and type "Coach Smith". The name shows in the transcript and in exported text.
- **Labels look wrong?** Tap **Re-run…** under the transcript and choose how many people were talking (2 is a good default for a one-on-one interview). Re-running replaces the transcript, quotes and posts. If it can't finish, your earlier ones are kept.
- Transcripts can have errors — names, numbers and unusual words especially. **Check anything you will quote against the audio.**

## 6. Best quotes

Each quote has a **score** (higher is more quotable) and a **time**. **Tap the time to play just that quote** — it jumps to the spot, plays it, and stops at the end (a large video may show "Loading" for a few seconds first).

Always listen before you use it. The quotes are suggestions.

How the quote check works, so you can trust the labels:

- Every quote is checked against the transcript. If the AI got the words wrong, the quote is **replaced with the exact words that were said**, or **dropped**. You'll never be shown words nobody said.
- A quote marked **"Adjusted to match the transcript word for word"** means the AI had skipped some words and the app restored them.
- The check confirms the *words* were said. It can't tell you whether a quote is **fair in context** — that's your call.

## 7. Social post drafts

Drafts are written in AP style from the strongest quotes. If a draft shows **"Check before posting: some wording in quotation marks isn't in the transcript word for word,"** fix the quote or the wording before you publish. Always read a draft as if you wrote it — it goes out under your name.

## 8. Get your work out

- **Share video / photo / audio** — sends you the original file to any app (messages, email, Drive). Very large videos can't use Share; use **Open in Drive** instead.
- **Export text** — the transcript, best quotes and posts as text you can paste or send.
- **Export whole project** — everything in a project together.
- Your original files are stored in the Google Drive folder the app created. They're yours; the app doesn't hide them.

## 9. Delete something

You can delete a recording or a project from its page. Deleting a recording removes it and what the app made from it (transcript, quotes, posts). *(Behind the scenes: after your transcript is saved, the transcription company's temporary copy is deleted too.)*

## 10. Recording people — be careful

Laws about recording conversations differ by state and situation. Some places require **everyone's** consent to record; others require only one person's. **When in doubt, tell people you're recording and get a clear yes on the recording.** Follow your newsroom's policy and the stricter rule if you're crossing state lines. This guide isn't legal advice.

## 11. Privacy at a glance

- Your recording is stored in Google Drive. The transcript and drafts are stored in the app's database.
- To produce the transcript and quotes, the audio is sent to a transcription service and the text to an AI service. The transcription service's copy is deleted as soon as ours is saved.
- Your recordings are not used to train anything we run. (Vendor policies: see the FAQ.)

## 12. If something goes wrong

| What you see | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| A video stopped on its own after about 30 seconds | The phone locked its screen. Most often Low Power Mode is on. | Keep the clip (don't tap Retake). Turn off Low Power Mode, or set Auto-Lock to 5 Minutes or Never, then record the rest. |
| **Try again** | An upload stopped partway. | Tap it. Don't re-record. |
| **Upload didn't finish** | An old upload was left half-done. | Tap **Try again**, or remove it if you don't need it. |
| **Saved, not transcribed** | The file is big; it was saved but not processed automatically. | **Transcribe now.** |
| A red message under a recording | Processing failed. Your file is safe. | Tap **Transcribe now** / **Re-run**. If it fails again, email us with the exact words on the screen. |
| Speaker labels are wrong | The AI guessed who spoke. | **Re-run…** with the right number of people, or rename speakers. |

**Still stuck?** Email **[support email]** with: what you were doing, the exact words on the screen (or a screenshot), and roughly what time it happened. We'll get back to you.
